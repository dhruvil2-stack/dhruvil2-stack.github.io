Each needed the other

On the ARVP RoboSub team I modelled our underwater transducers as circuits. One resonance branch missed the measured impedance by 18%, because a second, weaker mode near 65 kHz was hiding underneath. To find that mode I had to subtract the first one, and the first one couldn’t be measured cleanly until the second was out.

Peeling them apart, twice, in MATLAB brought the miss down to 3%.

How the peel works

A Butterworth–Van Dyke model treats a piezo transducer as a capacitor, C0, in parallel with one series R–L–C branch for each way it can vibrate.

  1. Read the big mode off the sweep. Conductance peaks at 151.7 kHz, so R1 = 1/G there, and C0 comes from the susceptance at the same point.
  2. Subtract it. Take C0 and branch 1 out of the measured admittance. What’s left is a hump near 65 kHz: the second mode.
  3. Fit the hump. Its height gives R2 = 1816 Ω and its low-side half-height width gives Q2 ≈ 1.06.
  4. Go back. Branch 2 was also inside the readings that set C0 and R1, so remove its share at 151.7 kHz, correct them (C0 4.46 → 4.69 nF), and peel again.
