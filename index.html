Underwater acoustic modem

ARVP RoboSub team · Jul 2026 – now

I’m on the team building an acoustic modem so ARVP’s submarines can send data to each other underwater. My part has been testing and modelling the piezo transducers, and designing the transmit circuit.

Why: Radio barely travels through water, so the subs have to talk using sound.

MATLAB · LTspice · Analog Discovery 2

Full write-up

The problem I ran into
The transducers are rated for 65 kHz, but in the pool they responded most strongly near 149 kHz. My first circuit model of them was also off by 18%.
How I fixed it
A hand calculation matched the 149 kHz peak to the tube vibrating along its length. I argued for testing carriers in the pool, which showed a usable band around 45–60 kHz, and I rebuilt the model with a second resonance.
What it improved
Model error dropped from 18% to 3%. With an accurate model, I sized a matching inductor that gets about 21 times more power into the transducer in simulation.

MP3 player

Personal project · 2026

An offline music player I designed from scratch: a 4-layer circuit board, the firmware and a 3D-printed case. It plays from an SD card and runs about 17 hours per charge.

Why: Paying for music streaming subscriptions for my family got too expensive, so I built my own player. I ended up using it every day.

ESP32 · C++ · Altium · Fusion 360

Full write-upWatch it play music

The problem I ran into
It kept resetting in the middle of songs. Measuring the 3.3 V supply showed it dipping by about 350 mV whenever I skipped a track.
How I fixed it
I wrote a Python script to measure the supply under five different loads, found that skipping was the only one that failed, and swapped the 22 µF bulk capacitor for 100 µF.
What it improved
The skipping test now passes. The heaviest case, full volume with rapid skipping, still dips, so it isn’t completely solved.

Ball and beam control system

Course project

A controller that balances a ball on a motor-driven beam, taken from measuring the motor through to running on real hardware.

Why: It was a controls course project. The goal was a controller that works on the actual hardware, and matches what the simulation predicts.

MATLAB · Simulink Real-Time

Full write-up

The problem I ran into
A controller is only as good as the motor model behind it, and I had to choose between controlling the motor by voltage or by current.
How I fixed it
I measured the motor on the bench until its torque constant and inertia matched the datasheet within about 5%, compared both approaches in simulation, and went with cascaded current and velocity loops plus a lead compensator for position.
What it improved
On hardware the ball settles in under 2 seconds with no steady-state error and under 10% overshoot, and it recovers from a disturbance in under 200 ms.

Verilog PID controller

Personal project

A PID controller written in Verilog with fixed-point math, plus a testbench that checks every output automatically against a Python reference model.

Why: I wanted to learn how a control loop is built in digital hardware, and how to test one properly.

Verilog · Vivado · Icarus · Python

Full write-up

The problem I ran into
It passed all 6,980 test vectors, but in a closed-loop simulation the output never settled. It kept swinging between about 82% and 105% of the target.
How I fixed it
The deadband logic was zeroing the output and wiping the integrator whenever the error got small, which kicked the system back out. I changed it to hold the output and freeze the integrator.
What it improved
The loop now settles to within ±0.4% of the target.
